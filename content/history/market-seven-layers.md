The roof of Santa Caterina Market gets the first look. The ground underneath deserves the second. Archaeologists excavating the market site recorded **seven broad phases** of occupation, from prehistoric activity to the market we know now.

The sequence goes roughly like this: prehistoric settlement; Roman pottery-making; a late-antique cemetery; occupation in the Andalusi and early comital periods; medieval urban growth; the Dominican convent of Santa Caterina; and, finally, the market. Seven chapters, one city block.

That Roman pottery workshop is a good reminder that this was not an empty fringe waiting for the medieval city to arrive. People used the site before the convent and before the market. Tombs found in the same area preserve another kind of history: the people who lived nearby also buried their dead here.

The convent stood for centuries and was eventually replaced by a market in the 19th century. Excavations tied to the modern market’s redevelopment covered around 7,000 square metres. A section of the former convent church and many Roman funerary structures were kept in the preserved archaeological area. The market is therefore a practical food stop above ground and a compressed neighborhood archive below it.

### A playful way to picture it

Imagine a layer cake, but each layer is a different use of the same plot: workshop, cemetery, streets, convent, market. No frosting. Probably a lot of pottery.
