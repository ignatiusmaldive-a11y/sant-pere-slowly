The archaeological site below the old Born market is a neighborhood frozen in the middle of an ordinary day—and then interrupted by a war. The excavations revealed about **8,000 square metres** of La Ribera: streets, houses, workshops and a stretch of the Rec Comtal.

Barcelona fell in 1714. From 1716, the Bourbon authorities ordered part of La Ribera demolished as the military citadel was built. The archaeological layer was preserved because the destruction abruptly stopped the everyday rebuilding that would normally erase old floors and walls. In a strange way, the cleared ground preserved a remarkably readable snapshot of the streets that had been there.

The city’s archaeology service records eight blocks and nine streets at the Born site. Researchers can match excavated buildings with archival records and, in some cases, identify the families who lived there. The story becomes specific: not just “a quarter disappeared,” but houses, shops, water channels and neighbors on named streets.

One caution: the Born site is a part of La Ribera’s story, not the whole neighborhood. And the image above is a siege plan, not a map of the later clearance. The map helps explain how the city was surrounded; the excavated street grid shows what residents lost.
